The Challenge

Astronauts manage their health alone, millions of miles from any doctor.

On the Moon and Mars, messages to Earth take many minutes each way. Crews must detect problems early, understand what they mean, and act on their own.

☢
RadiationInvisible high-energy particles that raise long-term cancer risk and damage the nervous system.
⚙
Bone & Muscle LossWithout gravity, bones shed density and muscles shrink rapidly in microgravity.
❤
Cardiovascular ChangesFluid shifts alter heart function, vision, blood pressure, and balance.
☾
Sleep DisruptionMicrogravity and 16 sunrises per day destroy the circadian rhythm.
👤
Isolation & StressMonths of confinement with the same small crew affects mood, cognition, and teamwork.
🌧
Cabin EnvironmentCO₂ build-up, pressure leaks, particulates, and noise all impact health silently.

      Clinical Measurement Guidelines

      Blood pressure

      Sit still for 5 minutes. Cuff at heart level on the upper arm. Stay quiet, take two readings a minute apart and enter the second.

      Heart rate and SpO₂

      Clip the oximeter on a warm fingertip. Hold your hand still and wait 30 seconds until the number stops changing.

      Blood sugar

      Wash hands, use a fresh lancet on the side of a fingertip, apply the drop to the strip. Note if you measured fasting.

      Temperature

      Use the ear or forehead thermometer, away from vents and after at least 15 minutes without exercise.

      Exercise plan

      Aim for about 2 hours a day, 6 days a week: resistive training (bones and muscle), treadmill (heart and legs), cycle ergometer (cardio). Add core and back work to protect the spine.

      General guidance for this prototype. Follow your flight surgeon’s protocol.
